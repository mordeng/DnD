Backstory
Longjohn Silver was born with the sky in his lungs and the sea in his veins. Raised in the floating citadel of Airspire, Silver's life was one of constant movement and change, much like the ever-shifting clouds of his home. From a young age, he showed an uncanny knack for taming and understanding the creatures of the air, a talent that would later define his path as a Beastmaster.

His life took a dramatic turn when he joined the crew of the Galeheart, a notorious pirate vessel famed for its daring escapades across the Azure Main. The Galeheart was more than a ship to Silver; it was a symbol of the unbridled freedom he so cherished, a small kingdom where every soul was equal under the unforgiving sun and the boundless sky.

For years, Silver sailed with the Galeheart, earning a reputation as a formidable hunter and a fearless fighter. Tavern brawls were his pastime, a way to feel the pulse of each new port and city, to taste the wild, unpredictable nature of life at sea.

But fate, as fickle as the wind, turned against Silver. One dark night, under a blood-red moon, the Galeheart was ambushed by a fleet of ruthless pirates, and a captain with a big scar on the left eye. His captain and crewmates were slaughtered, the ship plundered, and Silver was left for dead amidst the wreckage of his once proud vessel. The sea, once a symbol of freedom, became a grave for his friends and dreams.

After the merciless attack on the Galeheart left Longjohn Silver stranded, he found himself marooned on a desolate spit of land, a mere speck in the vastness of the ocean. The island was a cruel joke, offering life in the form of a dense jungle teeming with creatures, yet surrounded by an endless, unreachable horizon of water. His only companions were the sun, the sand, and the relentless tide.

In those first desperate days, survival was his only thought. Wounded, grieving, and alone, Silver was forced to hunt the very creatures he once felt kinship with. It was during one such hunt that he encountered a family of wild boars. Driven by hunger, he did what he had to do and took down the mother boar. As he prepared his grim meal, a small, frightened squeal caught his attention. There, hidden amongst the underbrush, was the smallest boar he'd ever seen, its eyes wide with fear and confusion.

Silver, with a heart heavy with loneliness and remorse, found himself unable to do the deed. Perhaps it was the boar's defiant will to live, or maybe a reflection of his own unyielding spirit, but he saw in that small creature a kindred soul, an ally in this unforgiving world. So, instead of ending its life, he chose to save it. He nursed the boar back to health, sharing his meager rations, and slowly, a bond formed between them, stronger and more profound than any he had known.

He named the boar Blacky, for the coarse, defiant hairs that stood on end along its spine. Together, they survived the island, relying on each other for companionship, protection, and sustenance. Blacky grew quickly, soon becoming a formidable creature in his own right, with tusks as sharp as cutlasses and a charge as powerful as the ocean's surge.

Silver realized that the boar was not just a companion but a symbol of his own indomitable will to survive and forge ahead. Blacky was a living testament to Silver's refusal to succumb to despair, a reminder that life, no matter how brutal or lonely, was still worth fighting for.

When they finally escaped the island, it was together, as captain and companion, a man and his beast against the world. They returned to the sea, not as victims, but as survivors, bound by a shared past and a future they would carve out together, one storm, one battle, one adventure at a time. Thus, the legend of Longjohn Silver and Blacky began, a tale of tragedy, survival, and an unbreakable bond forged in the heart of adversity.
