# Volume 3: "A Somewhat Unreliable Guide to Hell"

**Target**: ~100,000 words | 40 chapters | ~2,500 words/chapter

---

## Premise

Six friends descend into the first layer of Hell to rescue a city dragged there by infernal contract. What they find is a burning wasteland of eternal war, soul-powered vehicles, screaming currency, and a bureaucracy that makes Baldur's Gate look reasonable. Milo begins writing a travel guide. Each character faces a personal reckoning that demands a price. And somewhere in the sulfurous dark, a five-headed shadow is waiting.

## Key Adaptation Changes

- **`ratgeber.md` becomes literal**: Milo's in-universe "Somewhat Unreliable Guide to Avernus" provides blue-box chapter openers throughout. These are Adams-style worldbuilding delivered as travel guide entries.
- **Personal quests all resolve with cost**: No free wins. Every character pays something.
- **Tiamat reveal is a two-chapter planted-clue sequence**: Five colors, five shadows, five rings -- details scattered across the volume converge.
- **Soul coin economy drives survival plot**: Sanderson-style moral mechanics where the currency screams.

---

## The Guide Device

Every chapter opens with a blue-box excerpt from "The Somewhat Unreliable Guide to Avernus, by Milo Quickspark (Draft 1, Unfinished, Probably)". These provide:
- Adams-style worldbuilding humor
- Environmental/cultural context for the chapter
- Occasional foreshadowing disguised as comedy
- A contrast between Milo's analytical voice and the horror he's documenting

Example opening:
> [!NOTE] The Somewhat Unreliable Guide to Avernus -- Entry 7: "Weather"
> Avernus does not technically have weather. What it has is a series of atmospheric opinions, most of them hostile. The sky is a permanent shade of red that suggests the universe has been holding its breath for several millennia. Precipitation comes in four varieties: acid, rust, blood, and "other." The author recommends an umbrella for categories one through three, and a full suit of plate armor for category four.

## 5-Act Structure

### Act 1: Welcome to Hell (Chapters 1-8, ~20,000 words)

| Ch | Title (Working) | Content | Key Beats |
|----|----------------|---------|-----------|
| 1 | *Entry 1: Arrival Procedures* | Descent into Avernus. First impressions. The sky is wrong. The air is hostile. Milo begins taking notes. | Guide entry: "How to Enter Hell (Hint: The Hard Part is Leaving)" |
| 2 | *The Color of Wrong* | Environmental shock. Everything is subtly, persistently wrong. Fire burns differently. Shadows move independently. Lamil feels his wildfire dim. | Lamil's magic weakened. Sensory worldbuilding. |
| 3 | *Hellturel* | The chained city. Survivors clinging to buildings suspended over the Styx. The Companion burns black. | Scale and horror. The stakes made visual. |
| 4 | *The High Hall* | Exploring the ruined cathedral. Undead. Refugees. The first soul coins found. They scream when picked up. | Soul coin introduction through visceral experience. |
| 5 | *Interlude: Guide Entry -- "A Brief History of Avernus"* | Shorter chapter. Mostly Milo writing his guide, processing what he's seen through academic humor. The party rests and plans. | Breathing room. Comedy. |
| 6 | *Fort Knucklebone* | Arrival at Mad Maggie's. The fort described. Maggie herself -- eccentric, dangerous, "helpful." | Maggie is a great character. Let her be weird. |
| 7 | *The Price of Moving* | War Machine acquisition. First encounter with soul coin fuel dilemma. Mortal alternative: Lufnik volunteers, takes the vitality hit. | Moral mechanics introduced. Lufnik's sacrifice instinct. |
| 8 | *On the Road* | First drive through Avernus. Demon encounter. The world outside the fort is vast, hostile, and absurd. | Action. Worldbuilding. The Avernus road trip begins. |
