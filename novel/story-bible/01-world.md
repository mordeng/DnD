# World Bible

## Cosmology

The world operates on a planar cosmology where the mortal realm sits between celestial and infernal planes. The Nine Hells are a layered bureaucracy of evil -- a cosmic corporation run by Asmodeus, where suffering is both currency and product. The Abyss is its chaotic counterpart. Between them rages the Blood War, an eternal conflict that has been going on so long that neither side remembers what started it, which is the most realistic thing about it.

Gods exist. They are powerful, occasionally helpful, and universally terrible at clear communication.

## Arc 1 Locations: The Wild Edge

### Moreva
A remote tropical island ringed by treacherous reefs. Dense jungle interior, steep cliffs, hidden cave systems. The party washes ashore here after a shipwreck.

**Novel treatment**: Moreva is beautiful and deadly in equal measure. The jungle is alive in ways that suggest intelligence -- paths that shift, fruit that watches you eat it, insects that seem to be taking notes. A shadow dragon lurks beneath the island, feeding on the sanity of the inhabitants. The locals have been slowly losing their minds, which they attribute to bad weather, seasonal depression, and an unusually aggressive species of mosquito.

### The Mountain Pass
A brutal alpine crossing between Moreva's coastal region and the dwarven territories. Features griffin nesting grounds, a white dragon's lair (avoided), and increasingly hostile weather.

**Novel treatment**: The mountains are indifferent to human suffering in a way that Milo finds oddly comforting -- at least the mountains aren't *trying* to kill them. The cold is a character in itself: patient, thorough, democratic in its cruelty.

### Dwarf City of Zornstein
An underground dwarven metropolis built around a volcanic core. Normally a comfortable warmth; currently experiencing a catastrophic heating crisis caused by a magma elemental stealing power from a trapped fire god.

**Novel treatment**: Zornstein is everything a dwarf city should be -- magnificent engineering, excellent beer, and a deeply suspicious attitude toward anyone taller than four feet. The heating crisis provides both physical danger and an excuse for increasingly irritable dwarves.

## Arc 2 Locations: The City

### Baldur's Gate
A sprawling port city on the Sword Coast. Population ~40,000. Divided into Upper City (wealthy), Lower City (working class), and Outer City (everyone else). Governed by the Council of Four and the Grand Duke, enforced by the Flaming Fists mercenary company.

**Novel treatment**: Baldur's Gate is a city that runs on money, secrets, and the firm belief that everything has a price -- especially things that shouldn't. The political landscape is a knife fight in a phone booth, except the phone booth is on fire and everyone insists they're not holding a knife.

**Key locations**:
- **The Harbor**: Where legitimate commerce meets illegitimate commerce and they buy each other drinks
- **The Arena**: Underground fighting ring where the party earns money and reputation
- **The Elfensong Tavern**: Where Kalmaris and Longjohn end up drunk; serves as a recurring meeting point
- **The RR-Guild Headquarters**: Hidden beneath the city; site of Milo's captivity
- **The Mechanists' Collective**: Milo's guild; a warren of workshops, explosions, and optimism

## Arc 3 Locations: Hell

### Avernus (First Layer of the Nine Hells)
An endless burning wasteland where the Blood War rages. No natural day/night cycle -- the sky is eternally red, lit by falling meteors. The landscape shifts unpredictably. Rivers of blood and the memory-erasing River Styx cut through terrain made of bone, ash, and the compressed misery of the damned.

**Novel treatment**: Avernus is presented through Milo's "Somewhat Unreliable Guide" -- excerpts open chapters as blue boxes. The horror of Hell is real, but Milo's academic approach to documenting it creates dark comedy. The place is simultaneously terrifying and absurd: a cosmic war zone with traffic jams, wandering merchants, and a giant monster that mostly naps.

**Key features**:
- **No moon**: Critical for Lufnik's magic and emotional arc
- **Corrupted fire**: Lamil's wildfire magic is weakened/twisted
- **Soul coin economy**: Everything costs something, and that something used to be someone
- **Infernal War Machines**: Mad Max meets medieval Hell; vehicles powered by screaming coins

### Hellturel (Elturel in Avernus)
The holy city of Elturel, ripped from the mortal plane and suspended over the River Styx by massive chains. Its citizens are trapped, terrified, and slowly being corrupted. The Companion -- once a second sun that protected the city -- now burns with infernal light.

### Fort Knucklebone
Mad Maggie's junkyard fortress. Part trading post, part war machine garage, part madhouse. Maggie is a night hag who collects interesting things, including people. The fort smells of oil, brimstone, and poor life choices.

### The Bronze Citadel
Zariel's seat of power. A former palace redesigned as a weapon. Think corporate headquarters meets war room meets cathedral, all rendered in brass and malice.

### The Wandering Emporium
A traveling marketplace that appears wherever commerce is needed (and wallets are fullest). Run by Mahadi, a rakshasa who considers customer service a form of long-term predation.

## Environmental Rules

These apply consistently across the novel:

1. **Avernus corrupts**: Extended exposure darkens intentions, shortens tempers, amplifies worst impulses
2. **The Styx erases**: Contact with the river destroys memories; proximity causes confusion
3. **No natural sky**: Stars, moon, and sun are absent in Avernus; this affects moon-dependent magic
4. **Fire is wrong here**: Natural fire behaves normally; magical fire from the mortal realm is weakened; infernal fire is empowered
5. **Time is elastic**: Days may pass at different rates; the party shouldn't trust clocks

## Culture Notes

### Dwarven Culture (Zornstein)
- Craftsmanship is sacred; a poorly made item is a moral failing
- Beer is a food group and a diplomatic tool
- Hospitality is offered grudgingly but honored absolutely
- Admitting error is considered a weakness; elaborate justifications are an art form (Serox is a product of this culture)

### Baldur's Gate Society
- Everything is transactional; favors are currency
- The city runs on five things: trade, secrets, mercenaries, corruption, and the shared delusion that things are fine
- Social status is fluid -- money talks, blood whispers, and a good sword shouts

### Infernal Society
- Contracts are sacred; the letter of the law is everything, the spirit is nothing
- Hierarchy is absolute and violently enforced
- Demons (chaotic) and devils (lawful) despise each other; both despise mortals; mortals are too busy surviving to have opinions
