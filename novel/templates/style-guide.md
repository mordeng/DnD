# Style Guide

Prose rules, formatting standards, and Royal Road-specific guidance.

---

## Prose Rules

### Paragraph Length
- **Maximum**: 80 words (hard limit)
- **Ideal**: 40-60 words
- **Action sequences**: 20-40 words
- **Dialogue-heavy passages**: Short paragraphs, 1-3 sentences per beat
- **Milo's analytical passages**: Can push toward 80 but should still be broken by observation or humor

### Sentence Length
- Vary actively. Three short sentences followed by a long one. A long one followed by three short. Rhythm matters more than any single rule.
- **Milo comfortable**: Long compound sentences with semicolons and parenthetical observations
- **Milo scared**: Short. Declarative. No ornamentation.
- **Action**: Short sentences. Active verbs. No adverbs.

### Word Choice
- Prefer concrete over abstract ("the smell of sulfur" not "an unpleasant odor")
- Prefer specific over general ("obsidian" not "dark stone")
- Avoid adverbs unless the adverb IS the joke ("He said this entirely calmly while the wall behind him exploded")
- No modern slang unless deliberately deployed for humor
- Scientific vocabulary is Milo's voice -- use it, but make it accessible through context

### Dialogue

**Tags**: Use "said" for 90% of dialogue. The remaining 10%: action beats.
```
"That seems inadvisable," I said.
Serox crossed his arms. "Obviously, it's the only option."
```

**No said-bookisms**: Never use "exclaimed," "retorted," "intoned," "queried," "remarked," etc.

**Character identification**: Each character should be identifiable WITHOUT dialogue tags. Test: cover the names. Can you tell who's speaking?

**Action during speech**: Show what characters DO while talking. Serox evaluates things. Kalmaris gestures dramatically. Lamil goes still. Lufnik stands at attention. Longjohn drinks.

### Show vs. Tell

**Emotions**: Show through physical response, not labels.
- NO: "Longjohn was sad."
- YES: "Longjohn's hand found the empty space at his side where Blacky used to walk."

**Exposition**: Deliver through character reaction, not narrator lecture.
- NO: "Soul coins are the currency of Avernus, forged from damned souls on the third layer of Hell."
- YES: "The coin screamed when I picked it up. That is not, I should note, a metaphor."

## Formatting

### Scene Breaks
Use `* * *` centered on its own line. Only for time or location jumps within a chapter.

### Blue Boxes (Royal Road)
Render as blockquotes with a label. Used for:
1. **Alchemist's Notes** -- Milo's scientific observations
2. **The Somewhat Unreliable Guide to [X]** -- Travel guide entries (Volume 3)
3. **Letters & Documents** -- In-world correspondence
4. **Historical Asides** -- Brief lore

Format:
```markdown
> **Alchemist's Note:** [content]
```

Or for longer entries:
```markdown
> **The Somewhat Unreliable Guide to Avernus -- Entry 12: "Commerce"**
>
> [content spanning multiple paragraphs]
```

**Frequency**: 2-4 per chapter. Never more than 4.

**Rule**: Blue boxes must NEVER contain plot-critical information unavailable elsewhere. They are bonus content -- a reader who skips them should still follow the story.

### Emphasis
- *Italics* for internal thought, emphasis, and in-world document titles
- **Bold** sparingly, for extreme emphasis or blue box headers
- ALL CAPS never (except in Guide entries for comedic effect, rarely)

### Numbers
- Spell out one through twenty
- Use numerals for measurements and scientific data (Milo would)
- Spell out approximations ("about a hundred")

## Royal Road Specifics

### Chapter Titles
Format: `Chapter [Number]: [Title]`
- Titles should be intriguing, not spoilery
- Milo's academic voice in titles is a feature ("Chapter 7: Hypothesis and Hazard")
- Interlude chapters labeled as such

### Author's Notes
Every chapter gets one. 100-200 words. Placed at the end.
Content options:
- What inspired the chapter
- A behind-the-scenes detail about the D&D campaign (for flavor, not spoilers)
- A reader engagement question
- A tease for the next chapter
- A relevant joke or observation

Tone: Conversational, warm, grateful for readers. Never desperate or apologizing.

### Tags
Primary: Fantasy, Comedy, Adventure, Multiple Lead Characters
Secondary: Magic, Found Family, Male Lead, Isekai-Adjacent (technically they go to another plane), Progression (in a loose sense)

### Content Warnings
If a chapter contains:
- Character death: tag "Death" in author's note
- Body horror (Blobulus): tag "Body Horror"
- Grief: no tag needed, but acknowledge in author's note
- Violence: generally light enough not to need tags

## Tone Calibration

### The Humor-to-Serious Ratio

**Never pure comedy.** Even the funniest chapter needs one moment of genuine connection.

**Never pure drama.** Even Blacky's death chapter needs the warmth of the party's love for each other.

**The ideal chapter**: Makes you laugh at least once, makes you feel something real at least once, and makes you want to read the next one.

### Voice Consistency Checks

Ask after every chapter:
1. Does the narrator sound warm, witty, and omniscient -- not snarky, detached, or overly clever?
2. Does the prose shift subtly near different characters? (Scientific near Milo, sparse near Lamil, etc.)
3. Could I identify each speaking character without tags?
4. Is the humor emerging from character and situation, not from the narrator being smug?
5. Are emotional moments earned or forced?
6. Would this passage work read aloud? (If it stumbles verbally, rewrite it)

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

| Mistake | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Infodump paragraph | Break into dialogue, blue box, or character reaction |
| Combat play-by-play | Focus on stakes, fear, and choices, not mechanics |
| Everyone sounds the same | Read dialogue aloud in character voices |
| Jokes that undercut tension | Save humor for setup and denouement, not climax |
| Narrator becoming a character | The narrator observes and comments, never participates or has opinions on the plot's outcome |
| Head-hopping mid-scene | Stay near one character per scene; shift at scene breaks |
| Explaining the humor | If the joke needs explanation, rewrite the joke |
| Purple prose | If a description exceeds 3 sentences, cut it to 2 |
| Passive voice in action | "The sword was swung" -> "He swung the sword" |
